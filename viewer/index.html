How to read this

A simulated counter-drone site, replayed. Sensors report what they detect; the tracker turns those reports into tracks: where each object is, where it is heading and what it is. The map draws the tracker's picture over the truth, so you can see how close it got.

On the map

  • Where it really flew. The faint line is the true path, the cross is where the object is now. This is the answer sheet: the tracker never sees it.

  • The tracker's estimate. Dot: where it thinks the object is. Short line: where it is heading. Tail: its last 30 s. Label: track number and what it thinks the object is.

  • How sure it is. 95% likely to be inside the shaded area. Dashed when no sensor has updated the track for 2 s.

  • Not confirmed yet. A possible object still being checked. Most are radar clutter and vanish within seconds.

  • Radar hit. A full position: direction and distance.

  • Self-reported position. Airliners (ADS-B) and registered drones (Remote ID) broadcast where they are.

  • Camera hit. A direction only: a camera cannot tell how far away something is.

  • Radio direction finder. Hears a drone's control radio. Also a direction only.

  • Sensor site and radar reach. The triangle is the site, the big dashed circle how far its radar sees.

  • Camera view. Faint wedge: the wide camera. Bright narrow wedge: the zoom camera, which the tracker points at whatever it wants a closer look at.

Colours. Each real object has its own colour, and its tracks and sensor hits are drawn in it. Grey belongs to nothing real: clutter, or a false track. The colours come from scoring against the truth afterwards; the tracker does not know them.

Good tracking looks like

The dot sits on the cross, the tail follows the faint line, the shaded area stays small, the label is right, and each object keeps one track number the whole time.

Where to start

One site, from about 90 s. The intruder drone stops to hover, which hides it from the radar: radar ignores anything not moving toward or away from it, to filter out buildings and trees. The track holds anyway, on the cameras and the radio. Notes in the side panel point out moments like this.

Side panel and timeline

Tracks now: Class is what the tracker thinks the object is, ±95% its uncertainty in metres, Sensors the ones that updated it in the last 5 s. Hover a row or a dot for its error against the truth.

The graph above the time slider shows how wrong the whole picture is at each moment (GOSPA: position error in metres, plus a penalty per missed object or false track). Lower is better; click to jump.

Scores: per real object over the whole run: time tracked, average error, track breaks, and how long until it got the right label.
